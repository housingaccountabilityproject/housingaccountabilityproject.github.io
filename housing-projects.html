Q-015 · added November 6, 2026

What Is Planned for Craycroft and Lee Street?

Documented: The plan is to rehabilitate 82 existing homes: 74 apartments and eight duplex units. El Pueblo Housing Development (EPHD) also describes a Rental Assistance Demonstration conversion, which changes their subsidy structure.

Federal records show an environmental milestone and a submitted financing plan for the 82-unit preservation project. The RAD tracker reviewed November 7 lists “Financing Plan Submitted” without a closing date. Closing, proceeds and signed assistance contracts remain unverified.

Unknown: Which budget and bedroom count govern? The November presentation lists 124 bedrooms in its summary and 85 in its detailed slide. The summary cost entry is $33.9, without a millions label; the detail gives $34.7 million.

Next: Confirm the governing budget and bedroom count, then document public contract status, aggregate occupancy totals and published application and referral rules.

Source date: EPHD project page update August 6, 2026; presentation November 5, 2026, PDF pp. 12 and 31. [5, 6]

Q-016 · added November 6, 2026

What Assistance Is Authorized for El Portal Homes?

Documented: The August 18 PHA packet supports the up-to-120 El Portal voucher plan; its attached amendment is a public-comment draft, and the local action was adopted as amended. The October 30 memo estimates 181 planned voucher units across two phases, not delivered new homes. Separately, the City’s October 1 newsletter reports 41 households placed in existing El Portal apartments over the summer with Housing First assistance. That report does not establish implementation of the planned voucher expansion.

The memo forecasts about $1.15 million in additional annual rental revenue across both phases, subject to rent reasonableness. A second phase is anticipated in a subsequent plan year, subject to vouchers, program limits and required plan approvals.

Unknown: Final HUD approval, signed assistance contracts and dated totals of households receiving the new PBV assistance remain unverified. The October 20 PHA report anticipated related HUD approvals during October–December; that expectation is not approval. Final effective operating policies, completed inspections and rent determinations remain to be verified.

Next: Obtain aggregate counts of contracted units and households assisted, with reporting dates, inspection-completion totals and effective program policies. Do not request resident names, unit-level addresses or individual case records.

Source date: City memo October 30, 2026, pp. 1–2; it reports the August 18 plan approval. [7]

What Do County One Stop’s Results Tell Us?

The TPCH FY2026 scorecard reports 17 of 24 County One Stop rapid-rehousing leavers exiting to permanent housing (70.8%) for July 1, 2025–June 1, 2026, with no excluded leavers. A separate earlier group had 13 of 24 people return to homelessness within 12 months (54.2%); that cohort exited to permanent housing during June 1, 2024–June 1, 2025.

These are people in one project and two different cohorts, not a Countywide success rate or one group’s retention result. Matched reporting periods, inclusion rules and aggregate follow-up outcomes are needed before comparing costs or broader program performance.

Q-017 · added November 6, 2026

What Do Housing First’s Results Tell Us?

Documented: The FY2026 scorecard reports:

  • Did people leave for permanent housing? Yes, 21 of 36 leavers included in the calculation, or 58.3%, did. The period is July 1, 2025–June 1, 2026. The scorecard starts with 44 leavers and excludes eight based on their destinations. It does not measure everyone served.
  • How were openings filled? All 26 recorded openings were filled through Coordinated Entry in the same period. That describes the referral route into those openings, not access for everyone waiting.
  • Did people return to homelessness? Seven of 43 people who exited to permanent housing in the earlier June 1, 2024–June 1, 2025 period returned within 12 months, or 16.3%. This is a different group and reporting period.

Housing First’s 50/100 and MDOT’s 10/40 are weighted scores, not housing-success percentages. Both programs were exempt from the grant-expenditure measure. Their scores do not establish underspending.

Unknown: The aggregate destination categories accounting for the eight excluded exits are not established. Later aggregate outcomes and costs for comparable groups and reporting periods also remain unverified.

Next: Obtain an agency-prepared aggregate breakdown by broad destination category and updated outcome totals, with inclusion rules, denominators and reporting periods. Combine or suppress small groups where individuals could be identified; do not request participant records.

Source date: TPCH scorecard July 13, 2026; FY2026 methodology pp. 24–26. Exact cells and definitions appear in source 8 and source 9.

Research Standards

Plans, awards, commitments, expenditures, restricted balances, completed housing, and actual vacancies are separate measures. Published figures have limited reporting periods and may rely on historical agency statements. Questions are not findings of fraud or noncompliance.

Detailed source records remain in the Public Evidence Index. Corrections and additional official records can be shared through Contact.
