Where to get shelter help in Surprise

Native American Connections runs the West Valley Housing Assistance Center at 12779 W. Grand Ave. in Surprise. It lists housing navigation and assessment help Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Phoenix Rescue Mission operates eight shelter units for families at the site but handles shelter eligibility and intake off-site. Families with children under 18 can call the Family Housing Hub at 877-211-8661. For single adults, the operator lists the Brian Garcia Welcome Center in Phoenix at 602-229-5155.

Check the operator’s current instructions before going. The center’s resource hours are not walk-in shelter intake hours, and eight units is not a vacancy count.

Moreland Phase II

Arizona’s reservation lists show $2.5 million in annual low-income housing tax credits and a separate $3 million National Housing Trust Fund reservation for Moreland Phase II. The state lists 105 proposed apartments: 78 tax-credit units and 27 market-rate units. These reservations are separate from Phoenix’s authorization of up to $10 million and the proposal for 54 project-based vouchers.

Full context & qualifications

The records reviewed do not establish disbursement, voucher activation, or apartment availability.

Keep in mind. An older accomplishments summary labels Phase I with 237; do not use 237 as completed Phase I units. The February 2028 forecast reflects the June 2026 report. Current construction status and actual completion remain unverified.

Phoenix expands utility-aid authorization

On November 18, Phoenix authorized up to $100,000 more for emergency Arizona Public Service utility assistance through Wildfire, bringing the contract ceiling to $2,333,032. Staff estimated the increase could assist about 150 additional households.

Full context & qualifications

The vote does not show how much has been paid or how many households received assistance.

Laveen Park Place advances to financing proceedings

On November 18, Phoenix approved financing proceedings for up to $50 million in conduit notes and bonds for Laveen Park Place Phase I, which proposes about 216 rental apartments.

Full context & qualifications

This is a financing authorization, not a city grant. Issuance, the final number of income-restricted apartments and availability have not been verified.

What Phoenix’s 71,128 housing figure counts

Phoenix’s July–September 2026 Housing Phoenix Plan report lists 71,128 housing units created or preserved to date: 7,110 affordable units created, 6,363 affordable units preserved, 17,584 workforce units created and 40,071 market-rate units created. New-unit tracking begins in October 2019.

Full context & qualifications

This cumulative City-reported count does not establish current vacancies, households placed, net new housing supply or a deduplicated total. The published method does not explain how repeated preservation assistance or overlap across programs is counted.
