Research timeline

Two findings as checked November 14, 2026. The underlying records have their own dates.

  • Tucson & Pima County: City records separate the 30-unit motel conversion from the 59-unit Amazon Flats project; the 39 proposed project-based vouchers are not confirmed as under contract or available.
  • Phoenix & Maricopa County: The county’s draft household goal reconciles from 650 to 500 when the 150-household Tempe target is removed. That is a proposed planning change, not an observed loss of assistance.

Tucson & Pima County

Amazon Flats: existing and planned homes

The city describes a completed 30-unit motel conversion and a separate 59-unit new-construction project then projected for fall 2027. The city’s permit record documents inspection activity, not occupancy. A separate notice identifies 39 proposed project-based vouchers. We did not verify an executed voucher contract or available units for the new project. City project update · Permit and inspection record · Voucher notice

Housing Choice Voucher payments

The city’s FY2026 housing authority report shows $58,701,289 in assistance paid to owners and managers through the city-administered program, including $49,539,339 for Tucson and $9,161,950 for Pima County. This is July 2025–June 2026 payment activity, not the amount available for new applicants. The total is the sum of the report’s two rows. It is not directly comparable with prior-year audited federal expenditures. City housing authority report, p. 1

El Portal and proposed voucher policy

The October El Portal operating-policy draft gives a proposed policy date and ties implementation to site readiness. It is not an opening notice. The city’s separate public El Portal page stated it was not accepting waitlist applications when reviewed. A proposed county bond would provide $5 million toward capital work at 100 scattered-site units, with a $24 million estimated cost for that component. This is a funding request, not an award or an opening notice. Those 100 units are not the same measure as the city’s plan authority for up to 120 project-based vouchers. Operating-policy draft · Public intake page · Proposed bond worksheet · City memo on voucher plan authority

A separate voucher-plan proposal addresses bedroom subsidy standards and project-specific rent exceptions for new RAD voucher conversions. The city notice lists comments through November 30, 2026 and a December 1, 2026 hearing. These are proposals, not current changes to a household’s assistance. Official comment and hearing notice

Phoenix & Maricopa County

County HOME plan: proposed goals, not results

The county draft reduces the plan-period tenant-based rental assistance goal from 650 to 500 households, removing Tempe’s 150-household target. The draft also removes $240,000 in projected Tempe HOME program income. Neither figure measures households actually served or cash already lost. The roughly 14% forecast in the notice is a separate estimate. The county notice lists a November 19 hearing and comments through December 10, 2026. We did not verify adoption. County hearing notice · Redlined draft, see printed p. 186

Project awards and authorizations

For 25th at Bell, a state audit lists a $1 million conditional award, while a revised state program report lists $2.2 million for the same 53-unit project. The revised table calls the amount “Funding Amount,” and the 53 units are projected in that table. The documents we checked don’t reconcile the figures or establish what has been paid. For Marcos de Niza, a $100,000 city CDBG early-action authorization is separate from a $500,000 HUD planning grant. State audit, table 8 · Revised state report · City authorization · Phoenix grant reporting

From voucher to housing

The City of Phoenix reported that 56% of households it provided vouchers in 2022 went on to lease a unit. It reported an average of 114 days from voucher issuance to lease-up, not time spent on a waitlist. The 180 days was the search time then allowed, not an average wait or a claim about current policy. Tucson cites a 120-day average without identifying the cohort or measurement dates and starting and ending points. That isn’t a like-for-like comparison, and neither figure describes an applicant’s current odds. Phoenix staff report · Tucson ordinance resource

Voucher history by administering agency

Annual HUD snapshots, 2004–2025. These snapshots count units under contract for federal subsidy, not how many households found housing or how long they waited. Lease-up and wait-time data answer different questions. HUD counts units it classifies as available for occupancy; that is not a list of current openings. These are administering-agency counts, not yearly totals or citywide or countywide counts. Do not add the snapshots across years or add agencies together to estimate a region’s total. These counts do not rank agency performance or identify causes of change.

HUD-reported voucher program units

The marker does not identify a change in policy or program coverage.

Access to public records

HUD’s annual FOIA report lists a national backlog of 695 in FY2024 and 1,092 in FY2025. HUD’s separate performance narrative describes a reduction, but we did not establish a comparable baseline for that statement. FY2025 annual FOIA report (Word) · Chief FOIA Officer report · Separate performance report, pp. 79 and 84

HUD’s quarterly snapshots show the Region IX backlog rising from 11 to 44 across the four quarters of FY2026 (11, 29, 34, 44). The Q4 component rows total 1,593 nationwide. The quarterly figures are not replacements for the annual totals. Region IX covers multiple states and offices; the numbers do not measure Arizona alone or indicate what happened in any individual case. HUD quarterly report, p. 2 · Quarterly reporting definitions

To understand how often vouchers lead to housing, request aggregate counts of first-time tenant-based voucher issuances and completed leases over a common observation period. Ask for elapsed days and the allowed search period separately, with results broken out by administering authority where available. Don’t fill missing values with zero.

Key terms

See the project’s full glossary for other housing and funding terms.

Lease-up
When a household rents a unit using its voucher. Being issued a voucher does not mean the household has leased a home.
Lease-up rate
The share of a defined group issued a voucher that leased a unit during the stated follow-up period.
Voucher utilization
How much of an agency’s voucher capacity or funding is used. Check whether a report measures units or dollars before comparing figures.
CAPER
Consolidated Annual Performance and Evaluation Report. It describes how certain federal community development and housing funds were used and what results were reported for a program year.
