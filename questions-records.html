Posted: November 6, 2026, 7:42 a.m. MST (Tucson). Source dates are identified below.

When housing funding is announced, what happens next? Each answer separates what the records show from what still needs checking. “Unknown” means not established by the records reviewed here. A project’s status may have changed since its source date.

Q-018 · added November 6, 2026

Why does the project cost table not add up?

Documented: The November EPHD presentation’s project-cost entries sum to 590.8; its printed total is 593.5. Several entries omit the millions label. Its 1,326-unit total includes projects at different stages and rehabilitation work.

Unknown: The cost unit and total need clarification. The unit total alone does not show how many new homes were completed.

Next: Obtain a corrected project table with explicit cost units and separate planned, existing, rehabilitated and completed homes.

Source date: EPHD presentation November 5, 2026, PDF p. 12. [6]

What funding change is proposed for Housing First?

Documented: The submitted FY2026 priority listing names Housing First PSH for partial reallocation: $4,706,481 in annual renewal funding, $3,485,762 retained and $1,220,719 available for new projects. The Board cites the amount available for renewals in Tier 1 and says the applicant was notified June 25. This is a submitted funding decision, not a final HUD award. [12]

Next: Verify the final award and resulting service capacity. This named record does not identify the unnamed 50% case below.

Q-020 · added November 6, 2026

Which projects are behind the CoC funding decisions?

Documented: The FY2026 Continuum of Care application describes a 50% funding reallocation from one unnamed first-year project. Another unnamed project retained funding after documenting spending despite a lockout from HUD’s payment system.

Unknown: The passage names neither project and cannot be attributed to Housing First or MDOT. Final awards and implementation remain unverified.

Next: Match project IDs to the approved decisions and final award records.

Source date: FY2026 CoC application September 28, 2026, PDF p. 44. [10]

What still needs explaining in CDBG spending?

Documented: The corrected PR-26’s resources, spending and ending balance add up. Its $4,717,936.37 spending total exceeds the City’s $3,433,346.66 formula-CDBG narrative by $1,284,589.71. OMB’s reporting guidance separates formula CDBG from CDBG-CV; the $339,616.74 CV narrative amount belongs to a different report scope.

Unknown: The formula-CDBG difference remains unexplained. The earlier resource difference is resolved, and the separate public-service-limit correction does not reconcile these spending figures.

Next: Match formula-CDBG activities, funding categories and dates before drawing conclusions about spending or completed work. The companion CDBG review gives the detailed reconciliation.

Source date: City PY2024 CAPER; corrected PR-26 March 13, 2026, PDF pp. 13 and 148–150. OMB 2025 Compliance Supplement, PDF p. 61. [11, 15]

Q-017 · FY2026; measure-specific cohorts

What do Housing First’s results tell us?

Documented: Housing First recorded 21 permanent-housing exits among 36 counted leavers (58.3%), after excluding eight of 44 leavers, and all 26 openings filled through Coordinated Entry for July 1, 2025–June 1, 2026. Separately, seven of 43 people in the June 1, 2024–June 1, 2025 permanent-housing exit cohort returned within 12 months (16.3%). These are different groups and periods; weighted scores are not housing-success percentages or evidence of underspending.

Unknown: The eight excluded destinations are not identified. Later outcomes and costs for the same people and period also remain unverified.

Next: Obtain the destination breakdown and updated outcomes, keeping each group, period and cost comparison separate.

Research standards
