Tucson House: Floor-14 Temporary Certificate

City permit records list a Level 4 temporary certificate of occupancy for Tucson House’s 14th floor, with an expiration date of December 10, 2026. This certificate applies only to that floor. A separate building-final inspection on November 16 was marked Partial Pass. HUD’s November 3 property snapshot separately reports 358 total units and 357 assisted units.

Full context & qualifications

The 14th-floor permit records do not establish when residents moved onto that floor or when the whole building will be complete. The City’s October 16 announcement concerns the 15th floor.

County Housing Funding: What the Records Show

Pima County approved $8.7 million for housing and stability programs. A separate $1 million request was tabled indefinitely. The County also approved $2.99 million in PRICE agreement scopes for home repairs and eviction assistance. These approvals and targets do not establish actual County spending or households served.

Full context & qualifications

Pima County approved $8.7 million for housing and stability programs. A separate $1 million request was tabled indefinitely. The County also approved $2.99 million in PRICE agreement scopes for home repairs and eviction assistance. These approvals and targets do not establish actual County spending or households served.

On September 8, 2026, the Board approved $5 million for gap funding and $3.7 million for housing stability, totaling $8.7 million for FY2027. The separate additional $1 million request was tabled indefinitely. These are allocation decisions, not evidence of payments or completed assistance. The posted meeting summary expressly is not approved minutes. Meeting summary, PDF p. 6 · Official allocation action

The two County-approved PRICE agreements total $2,986,331: $2,009,932 for home repairs and $976,399 for eviction-prevention and legal services. Their stated term is July 1, 2026–June 30, 2029. These are components of existing PRICE grant funding, not additional federal revenue to add to the full award. Repair agreement · Eviction-prevention agreement · Repair approval · Eviction-prevention approval

The records identify planned work and targets. They do not establish completed repairs, actual payments, legal outcomes or households already assisted. The posted execution pages contain blank mayor, Board chair and clerk signature lines; attorney review is present. Fully executed counterparts and later amendments remain unverified. Blank lines in a posted packet do not prove that no agreement was later executed.

Separately, USAspending reports $78,172.27 in cumulative federal outlays for the City of Tucson’s $11,519,567.21 PRICE award, B-23-PR-04-0001. In the November 8 research snapshot, the latest returned reporting period was August 2026, before the September County approvals. This is not a measure of County spending. Monthly cumulative outlay rows must not be added together. The database’s later update stamp is not the expenditure cutoff. Federal award and reporting-period record

Later expenditures and actual household outcomes remain unverified. The difference between the award and reported outlays is not a verified available balance, and these records alone do not establish improper withholding or misuse.

Craycroft and Lee Street: Council Vote Confirmed

The November 4, 2026 legal action report records a 6–0 vote adopting Resolution 24206 for Craycroft Towers and Lee Street Duplexes. Consent Agenda Item J was considered separately; Cunningham was absent/excused. This confirms the vote on the previously reported authorization for up to $10 million in bonds.

Full context & qualifications

The vote does not establish bond closing, proceeds received, spending or completed housing. Signed assistance contracts, current vacancies and the conflicting bedroom and cost estimates remain unverified. The volume-cap allocation and bond authorization concern the same financing and must not be added together.
