Tucson’s CDBG Funding: What Adds Up, and What Still Needs Explaining

We withdrew the earlier $944,972.60 comparison because it combined regular CDBG and CDBG-CV, which are reported separately. The $1,284,589.71 difference within regular CDBG remains unexplained; it does not establish missing funds.

Summary revised: November 6, 2026, 9:52 a.m. MST (Tucson). Original correction history and source dates follow unchanged.

Posted: November 6, 2026, 7:42 a.m. MST (Tucson). Source dates are identified below.

What Did We Find, and Why Does It Matter?

The resource figures and ending balance in Tucson’s CDBG reports add up. The remaining question is more focused: how does the City’s spending narrative connect to the corrected financial report for its regular CDBG program? Federal guidance now helps separate that question from coronavirus-response funding. Financial summary · Spending narrative · OMB reporting guidance, PDF p. 61

For residents, the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) can mean home repairs, better public facilities and services for low-income people. Connecting the dollars to completed work helps show what that funding delivered. City’s description of CDBG-supported work

What Adds Up?

Program income, money generated by CDBG-funded activities, accounts for the previously questioned resource difference. The full financial report also includes money carried over from the previous year:

  • $5,068,664 annual grant + $349,468 program income = $5,418,132. The income is already included; adding it again would double-count it.
  • $3,778,221.56 opening balance + $5,418,132 grant and income = $9,196,353.56 in total resources.
  • $9,196,353.56 resources − $4,717,936.37 expenditures = $4,478,417.19 ending balance.

Those calculations reproduce the City’s resource total and ending balance. The reported balance does not establish money available for new work today. Corrected PR-26, PDF p. 149

Which Spending Figures Should We Compare?

The Office of Management and Budget’s 2025 Compliance Supplement says the standard PR26 CDBG Financial Summary Report has been restricted to annual formula grants since September 14, 2020. A separate PR26 report covers CDBG-CV, the CARES Act coronavirus-response grants. OMB reporting guidance, PDF p. 61

That makes the regular CDBG figures the appropriate starting point:

  • Corrected standard PR-26 expenditures: $4,717,936.37.
  • CDBG spending stated in the narrative: $3,433,346.66.
  • Difference requiring reconciliation: $1,284,589.71.

The summary table’s $4,717,936 agrees with the corrected PR-26 after rounding. The narrative says the final CDBG draw occurred after June 30, 2025, which may help explain the difference. But the records reviewed do not show which transactions connect the two figures or establish that timing explains the full amount. Summary table and narrative, PDF p. 13 · Corrected PR-26, PDF p. 149

Why Set Aside the Earlier $944,972.60 Comparison?

That calculation subtracted both the $3,433,346.66 CDBG narrative and the $339,616.74 CDBG-CV narrative from the rounded $4,717,936 summary total. It mixed the separately reported grant programs. In light of the federal reporting guidance, it should not be presented as a second comparable spending gap. The question to pursue is the $1,284,589.71 difference within the regular CDBG figures. City narrative · OMB reporting guidance

Does the City’s Separate Correction Resolve This?

The City says $102,106.09 in public-service costs was assigned to PY2024 instead of PY2023, and that its HUD field representative approved the correction. We reviewed that City statement, not a separate HUD approval record.

The adjustment appears on line 30 of the PR-26, in the public-service-limit calculation. Line 14, the adjustment to total expenditures, is zero. Subtracting the public-service adjustment from total spending would treat a cap adjustment as an adjustment to total expenditures. This correction does not, by itself, explain the narrative difference. Correction explanation and PR-26, PDF pp. 148–149

What Would Make the Picture Clearer?

The corrected PR-26 is available and includes activity and voucher detail. The next step is to connect those records to:

  • A line-by-line explanation bridging the $3,433,346.66 CDBG narrative to the $4,717,936.37 corrected total, identifying transactions recorded after year-end and keeping CDBG-CV separate.
  • Commitments, restrictions and later transactions affecting the reported balance.
  • Completed work and aggregate beneficiary counts from the same reporting period. PR-26 activity detail, PDF p. 150

How Far Do These Findings Go?

The $1,284,589.71 is an arithmetic difference between reported amounts that still needs a transaction-level explanation. This review does not establish missing funds, currently available assistance or a cost per housing outcome.
