Research updates and evidence notes

Initial release: November 2, 2026 · First research review and PR-26 source correction: November 4, 2026 · Original source dates remain specific to each finding.

These notes summarize public records. Reported resources, project commitments, expenditures, and available housing units are different measures. They must not be treated as interchangeable.

Housing plans: confirmed actions and open questions

Review snapshot: November 5, 2026, 8:39 a.m. MST (Tucson, UTC−7). Original source dates differ.

Next update planned: November 5, 2026, noon Tucson time (MST).

El Portal voucher expansion

The August 18 PHA Legal Action Report records an 8–0 vote adopting PHA2026-56 as amended, approving plan changes to allow noncompetitive PBV allocation for units owned by the PHA and its nonprofit subsidiary, EPHD. Legal Action Report

The separate October 30 memo describes up to 120 El Portal PBV units in the 2026 plan year, with 181 planned across two phases and approximately $1.15 million in additional annual rent revenue, subject to rent reasonableness. Implementation depends on vouchers, approvals, inspections, and eligibility reviews. These are plans, not confirmed placements or vacancies. Memo, pages 2–3

The policy package also describes 70 or more additional units in 2027. Phase sizes need clarification: 120 is a cap, not a confirmed first-phase count. Its December 1 effective date appears in a document still marked REVIEW AND APPROVAL DRAFT; adoption is not established. Policy package, PDF pages 3 and 5

Craycroft figures need reconciliation

The November 5 presentation lists 82 units on two slides, but 124 versus 85 bedrooms. An August environmental review estimates $33,894,857, consistent with the pipeline table’s rounded “$33.9” entry. The detailed November slide gives $34.7 million. These may reflect different estimates or scopes; their relationship needs clarification. Environmental review, page 4 · Presentation, pages 12 and 31

The pipeline’s displayed costs sum to 590.8, versus its printed 593.5. If all entries mean millions, that is a $2.7 million arithmetic difference. The table needs clarification; this is not evidence of missing money or overruns.

Bond capacity documented

Arizona Finance Authority’s October 7 tracker records a $10 million volume-cap allocation, approved February 9, expiring December 15. Issue-price and closing-date fields are blank in that snapshot. This is financing capacity, not a grant, proof of issuance, or confirmation of the November 4 City vote. Tracker, project BX260130022

Community preference remains in development

The City says no Community Preference Policy or predetermined design exists yet. Recommendations are targeted for May–June 2027, not a current preference for applicants. Presentation, pages 3 and 10

CEHD meets November 5, 4–6 p.m. Tucson time. This briefing precedes that meeting and does not imply approval of its proposals. Agenda

November 5, 2026 — Verified allocations, County preservation funding, and CDBG reporting

Next update planned: November 5, 2026, noon Tucson time (MST).

Source verification snapshot: November 5, 2026, 7:58 a.m. MST (Tucson, UTC−7) · Second November 5 update · Not newly announced funding · Source periods identified below

HUD allocation figures independently confirmed

HUD’s FY2026 formula-allocation workbook lists CDBG $4,962,047; HOME $3,061,840.81; ESG $448,998; and HOPWA $1,140,597 for Tucson. These amounts total $9,613,482.81 and match the City’s FY2027/PY2026 Annual Action Plan. HOME covers the Tucson/Pima County consortium. These figures were already recorded in our research; today’s check strengthens their documentation rather than identifying additional funding.

These are allocations, not verified receipts, expenditures, executed grant agreements, or assistance currently available to an individual household. The City plan separately projects $700,000 in program income; that is not an additional federal allocation or confirmed collected revenue. The plan states that it is subject to HUD review and approval.

Sources: HUD FY2026 allocation workbook, sheet “FY26 CPD Formula Allocations,” Tucson row 41; City Annual Action Plan, executive summary pp. 2–3 and AP-15 pp. 29–32. Despite its filename, the City document’s cover identifies FY2027/PY2026, July 1, 2026–June 30, 2027.

A separate $500,000 County award for Craycroft and Lee Street

Pima County’s February 17, 2026 announcement reports a 4–1 Board of Supervisors vote approving a nine-project, $5 million funding round. It identifies $500,000 for El Pueblo Housing Development’s preservation of 82 existing apartments at Craycroft Towers and Lee Street Duplexes.

This documents an approved County award, not disbursement, completed rehabilitation, or current vacancies. It is distinct from the City’s reported $1.5 million CDBG reallocation and the bond proposal below; these measures should not be combined into a claimed amount spent on the properties.

Source: Pima County’s February 17 funding announcement, “Existing, preservation.” This is an older award newly incorporated into this website update.

November 4 bond proposal: outcome remains unverified

The City’s November 4 council agenda included proposed authorization for up to $10 million in aggregate principal amount of multifamily housing revenue bonds for Craycroft Towers and Lee Street Duplexes. We have verified the agenda item, but not its adoption or subsequent bond issuance. The official action summary and minutes were unavailable when checked on November 5. An agenda proposal does not establish that financing closed, proceeds were spent, or apartments became available.

Source: Official November 4 council agenda, item 7(j), NOV04-26-315, proposed Resolution 24206. Status: proposal documented; outcome awaiting official corroboration.

Clarifying the corrected CDBG financial summary

Fresh review of the original PY2024 CAPER confirms the corrected PR-26’s $4,717,936.37 expenditure total. The previously published $944,972.60 and $1,284,589.71 comparisons remain distinct calculations, not findings of missing money.

The −$102,106.09 adjustment appears in the public-service-cap calculation on line 30. Line 14’s total-expenditure adjustment is $0. The cap adjustment should not be subtracted from total expenditures or assumed to explain either narrative comparison.

The CAPER includes activity and voucher detail. What remains needed is a complete reconciliation connecting the relevant activities, reporting periods, classifications, and published totals.

HUD’s separate CDBG-CV instructions describe cumulative life-of-grant expenditure reporting, unlike the annual formula CDBG reporting framework. The labels and periods of Tucson’s particular figures must be verified before comparing them; this general distinction does not itself resolve their differences.

Sources: City PY2024 CAPER, PDF pp. 13 and 148–150, corrected PR-26 dated March 13, 2026; HUD CDBG-CV PR-26 instructions, PDF p. 2. Finding F-007 · Question Q-007.

November 5, 2026 — Continuing the CDBG financial review

Research update · Existing findings and source-status clarification

Following the November 4 publication of Tucson Housing Funding and Outcomes, the Housing Accountability Project is preparing a focused review of Tucson’s Community Development Block Grant expenditure reporting.

The corrected Tucson PR-26 Financial Summary has been located within the City’s PY2024 CAPER on PDF page 149. The remaining question is how its expenditure total connects to the CAPER’s CR-15 table and narrative figures.

Our review keeps two calculations separate:

  • $944,972.60: the rounded CR-15 expenditure total, less the standard CDBG and CDBG-CV narrative amounts.
  • $1,284,589.71: the corrected PR-26 expenditure total, less the standard CDBG narrative amount alone.

These comparisons use different inputs. Their accounting scopes have not been established as equivalent, and neither calculation establishes missing money or improper spending.

The next stage is to examine the reporting periods, CDBG-CV classification, activity-level transactions, and adjustments needed to explain the relationship between these figures.

Open research questions

The following are unresolved and are not allegations of misconduct.

  • How does the PY2024 CAPER CR-15 CDBG expenditure total ($4,717,936) reconcile to the narrative CDBG amount ($3,433,346.66) and the separately stated CDBG-CV amount ($339,616.74)? Subtracting both narrative figures from the table amount produces $944,972.60 in arithmetic difference, but the categories may not use equivalent accounting scopes. Separately, a $1,284,589.71 comparison in the research workbook uses the corrected PR-26 expenditure total ($4,717,936.37), located in the CAPER on November 4, and only the CDBG narrative. The corrected Tucson PR-26 was subsequently located and directly inspected in the City's CAPER (PDF p. 149); the $4,717,936.37 total is therefore source-visible, although the comparison remains unreconciled. Neither difference establishes missing funds. See Finding F-007 and Question Q-007.
  • What do the relevant IDIS activity-level commitments and draw records establish?
  • What are the current contracted, occupied, and vacant PBV counts, as of a specific date?
