Phoenix and Maricopa County housing glossary

Plain-language terms for reading the funding, project and outcome records.

Housing terms, public research labels and agency/program acronyms. Some concepts appear in more than one group.

Research snapshot: November 8, 2026.

Plain-language explanations of housing terms, public source labels and official agency identifiers. Examples retain their source’s program, period and status; they are not eligibility or legal advice.

How to read codes and identifiers

Codes identify records; acronyms shorten names. Check who assigned a code and whether it identifies a source, a finding, a program, an agency or a grant. Website labels are not agency-created codes. No identifier by itself proves payment, completion or compliance.

Website reference labels

Phoenix source IDs

Reference labels assigned by Housing Accountability Project to sources cited in the public Phoenix records index. The prefix groups source references; the final number distinguishes entries.

Observed examples: PHX-CITY-002; PHX-EXT-PROJECT-210; PHX-CFP-011

Keep in mind: These are website reference labels, not HUD grant IDs, City project codes or verification scores. Open the cited source and check its title, date, scope and status.

Find these labels in the public Phoenix records index.

Phoenix finding and note IDs

Website labels for published statements, evidence entries or explanatory notes, rather than the source documents themselves.

Observed examples: PHX-WEB-002; PHX-CFP-WEB-001; BELL-03; HOME-dataset-vintage

Keep in mind: Follow the linked source IDs. A claim ID is not a grant number or proof of approval, payment or independent verification.

Find these labels in the public Phoenix records index.

Phoenix question and evidence-need IDs

Website navigation labels for open research questions and specific evidence needs.

Observed examples: question-overview-01; question-bell-01; capital-evidence-needed-01

Keep in mind: An evidence need is not a submitted records request, agency tracking number or finding of wrongdoing.

Find these labels in the public Phoenix records index.

Official identifiers and source locations

HUD housing-agency code

The agency code that HUD’s Capital Fund workbook pairs with City of Phoenix Housing Department.

Observed examples: AZ001

Keep in mind: AZ001 identifies the agency in this system, not a HOME grantee ID or a particular property. Do not decode the digits beyond the source mapping.

[1]

HUD Capital Fund grant number

The exact grant identifier paired with Phoenix’s federal grant-year 2026 Capital Fund award in the source schedule.

Observed examples: AZ20P00150126

Keep in mind: Preserve the whole code. Do not infer cash receipt, expenditure, or a meaning for each substring from the identifier.

[1] [2]

Assistance Listing Number (ALN)

A federal-program classification shown in the SEFA’s Federal Assistance Listing Number column.

Observed examples: 14.872 — Public Housing Capital Fund; 14.239 — HOME Investment Partnerships; 14.218 — CDBG Entitlement Grants

Keep in mind: A program number is not an individual grant, project, payment or grant year. Multiple grants/activities can fall under one ALN.

[3]

HUD HOME grantee and activity IDs

These fields identify the reporting grantee and individual IDIS activity in the HOME dataset. Keep the grantee and activity number together.

Observed examples: PHOENIX: GRANTEE_ID 10659 / IDIS_ACTV_ID 4938; ARIZONA: GRANTEE_ID 136 / IDIS_ACTV_ID 7160

Keep in mind: Neither code is an apartment number. Different funder activities can describe the same property, and their assisted-unit subsets can overlap. The raw HOME dictionary has CDBG wording in some grantee-field descriptions; use actual HOME record context, not that wording to relabel the program.

[4] [5]

HUD record identifiers

ID_CHAR is the character-form unique record identifier described in the HOME dictionary; OBJECTID is the service’s object-row field.

Observed examples: ID_CHAR 900000001055151; ID_CHAR 900000001039206

Keep in mind: These identify records in a particular dataset, not physical housing units, parcels or an assistance amount. Do not invent a digit-by-digit decoding or treat an object-row ID as universally stable.

[6] [7]

Council records and enacted-action identifiers

Legistar metadata and staff-text records use distinct matter, file and text identifiers. The ordinance number identifies the named Council ordinance.

Observed examples: MatterId 35197; MatterFile 26-1162; MatterTextId 35297; Ordinance S-53026

Keep in mind: A staff-text request does not prove adoption. Check action metadata/status/date. These are not grant, property or payment IDs; do not parse numeric segments into unverified dates.

[8] [9]

City capital-project codes

City CIP schedule identifiers for separately described capital-project funding rows.

Observed examples: AH20620043 — 25th at Bell ARPA project; AH30300013 — CPLC The Bell HOME / Community Housing Development Organization (CHDO) project

Keep in mind: CIP means Capital Improvement Program. Two codes at one property do not create two developments; a capital-plan figure is not proof of payment. The internal characters are not decoded here.

[10]

Arizona LIHTC project number

The Arizona LIHTC master’s project number for the Bell entry. Keep the leading zero.

Observed examples: 0940 — 25th & Bell Apartments

Keep in mind: LIHTC means Low Income Housing Tax Credit. This code is not a parcel number, deed, current owner or affordability-expiry date.

[11]

City dedication file number

The City easement staff report lists this file for Bell’s drainage easement at 16801 N. 25th St.

Observed examples: FN 210078

Keep in mind: A dedication-file number is not an assessor parcel number or restriction expiry. The letters FN are retained as printed, without an unsupported expansion.

[12]

Census geography, table and estimate codes

The retained Census row is Phoenix city; B25064 is the median-gross-rent table, E001 its estimate and M001 its margin of error.

Observed examples: GEO_ID 1600000US0455000; B25064_E001; B25064_M001

Keep in mind: Geography code, table/variable code and local PHX-CITY-007 source label are different identifiers. Phoenix city is not the metro area. Do not infer a current asking rent from the survey.

[13]

HUD HOME data field names

The dictionary labels these Total Units, Total HOME Units, Total HOME Funding Amount and Activity Completion Date.

Observed examples: TOTAL_UNITS; TOTAL_HOME_UNITS; TOTAL_HOME_FUND; COMPLETED_DT

Keep in mind: The HOME units are a subset; funding is not independently verified cash payment; administrative completion is not an inspected occupancy or construction date.

[14]

Source locations, not program IDs

A page or sheet-and-cell reference tells readers where to inspect the cited source.

Observed examples: PDF page 49 / printed page 49; Grants!A218:D218; PHA-Level Funding!A221:F221

Keep in mind: PDF page numbering can differ from printed page numbering. A workbook sheet/range is not a grant number, payment code or count of records.

Find these labels in the public Phoenix records index.

Program and reporting acronyms

These names identify a program, organization or report. They do not tell you whether an amount is planned, authorized, awarded, drawn or spent.

HUD

U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development.

The federal agency; its name is not a funding stage.

[15]

HOME

HOME Investment Partnerships Program.

Keep HOME-designated units within the property total; keep the funding field separate from payment evidence.

[16]

CAPER / AAP

Consolidated Annual Performance and Evaluation Report / Annual Action Plan.

CAPER reports performance; the AAP plans uses. Annual Action Plan is distinct from a PHA Annual Agency Plan. Preserve draft/final status and period.

[17]

AMI

Area median income, an income benchmark used by housing programs.

Use the applicable income limits for the household size, year and area; a percentage of AMI is not a percentage of rent.

[18]

PHA

Public housing agency.

An agency administering public housing or rental assistance. A PHA code identifies the agency, not a particular grant or apartment.

[19] [20]

CFP

Public Housing Capital Fund Program.

A HUD program for public-housing capital needs. CFP is distinct from CPF, Community Project Funding, a reversed-letter abbreviation appearing in the CAPER.

[21] [22]

ACC

Annual Contributions Contract.

A HUD/PHA agreement. A published award schedule or unexecuted form does not establish a signed Phoenix agreement.

[23] [24]

ALN

Assistance Listing Number.

A federal-program identifier used in the SEFA. ALN 14.872 identifies the Public Housing Capital Fund; it is not a transaction ID.

[25]

LIHTC

Low-Income Housing Tax Credit.

A housing tax-credit financing program. A LIHTC project number is a program record identifier, not a parcel number.

[26] [27]

HCV / PBV

Housing Choice Voucher / Project-Based Voucher.

Tenant-based assistance generally follows the eligible household to a qualifying rental; PBV assistance is attached to specified apartments. An authorization does not establish an executed contract or vacancy.

[28] [29] [30]

HAP

Housing Assistance Payments.

In program records, HAP refers to rental-assistance contracts/payments. In this website’s name, HAP means Housing Accountability Project. Read the context.

[31]

IDIS

Integrated Disbursement and Information System.

HUD’s system for community-development and housing grant activities. Keep grantee and activity identifiers together.

[32]

LOCCS

Line of Credit Control System.

HUD’s grant draw system. Capital Funds vouchered through LOCCS and a PHA payment to a vendor are different steps.

[33]

SEFA

Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards.

An accounting-period schedule, not a cash-flow statement or a grant-by-grant payment ledger.

[34]

ARPA / HOME-ARP

American Rescue Plan Act / the HOME American Rescue Plan program.

These labels are not interchangeable. Bell’s cited ARPA assistance must not be relabeled HOME-ARP.

[35] [36]

CDBG / NSP

Community Development Block Grant / Neighborhood Stabilization Program.

Separate named funding programs. A mixed-source authorization does not establish how much came from each.

[37] [38] [39]

FY / PY / CY

Fiscal year / program year / calendar year.

Match the dates stated in each source. City fiscal years, federal grant years and program years are not automatically the same period.

[40] [41] [42]

CIP / GO

Capital Improvement Program / general obligation.

CIP identifies the City’s multi-year capital plan; GO identifies the type of bond financing. A programmed amount is not proof of an award or payment.

[43] [44]

IDA

Industrial Development Authority.

Phoenix IDA is a public issuer involved in conduit financing. Issuer-reported closing amounts are not City spending.

[45] [46]

AHGB

Assisted Housing Governing Board.

A named local governing body. Local approval does not by itself establish HUD approval.

[47]

CPLC

Chicanos Por La Causa, Inc.

The organization named in Bell’s 2020 HOME authorization. That historical role does not establish current deed ownership.

[48]

DDTF

Demolition and Disposition Transitional Funding.

A term in HUD Capital Fund formula guidance; it is not an additional Phoenix payment established by the glossary.

[49]

EPIC

HUD’s system used here for Capital Fund five-year action plans and budgets.

This guide uses the verified function of the system; it does not infer a full name from the initials.

[50]

ACS / MOE

American Community Survey / margin of error.

ACS values are survey estimates for a stated geography and population. Read the estimate together with its confidence level and MOE.

[51] [13]

CHAS

Comprehensive Housing Affordability Strategy data.

HUD’s special Census tabulations used for housing-needs analysis; retain the dataset period and geography.

[52]

CoC / PIT

Continuum of Care / Point-in-Time count.

A homelessness planning/program framework and a count for a specified time. Maricopa regional evidence is not a Phoenix-city-only count.

[53]

Keep each city’s public references in context

Phoenix PHX-* labels and Tucson’s public source, finding and question labels belong to separate research areas. Follow each label’s title and cited source; matching numbers do not establish a connection. See the Tucson glossary for the public labels used there.

Housing and funding terms

Affordable unit

An affordability label describes a source’s housing category or stated income or rent criteria. Its meaning depends on the program and measure.

Phoenix example: The draft FY2025–26 CAPER describes 11 HOME-designated apartments at 25th @ Bell for households at or below 40% of area median income.

Keep in mind: A label alone does not establish a legally enforceable restriction. A project-wide apartment count does not mean every apartment has the same income limit.

[54] [55]

Area median income or AMI

An income benchmark used to describe housing eligibility and set-asides. A percentage of AMI is an income threshold, not a percentage of a tenant’s rent.

Phoenix example: The draft CAPER states an at-or-below-40% AMI threshold for the HOME-designated units at Bell, Pueblo and Harmony III.

Keep in mind: A percentage alone does not establish a household’s eligibility or exact rent. Use the applicable program’s limits for the household size, year and area.

[54] [55] [18]

CAPER

The Consolidated Annual Performance and Evaluation Report describes a jurisdiction’s reported performance under its housing and community-development programs.

Phoenix example: Phoenix’s draft FY2025–26 CAPER reports five completed rental developments totaling 556 apartments.

Keep in mind: Keep the draft label. Reported completion does not establish occupancy, a certificate of occupancy or HUD acceptance.

[54] [55] [56]

HOME

The HOME Investment Partnerships Program is a federal housing-assistance program. These records identify HOME funding and designated units within developments.

Phoenix example: The Bell records include separate Phoenix and Arizona HOME activities at the same development.

Keep in mind: The jurisdiction, activity number, unit count and financial field must stay attached to each entry.

[57] [58] [59] [60] [16]

HOME designated unit

An apartment identified in the source as part of the development’s HOME-assisted subset.

Phoenix example: The draft CAPER reports 49 HOME-designated units within the five developments’ 556 apartments.

Keep in mind: The 49 are included in the apartment total. They are not additional apartments or a verified count funded exclusively by Phoenix.

[54] [55]

Physical development

The housing property being counted, as distinct from the financing records associated with it.

Phoenix example: Phoenix and Arizona HUD activities both identify 53 total units at 25th @ Bell, at the same address.

Keep in mind: Two activity records do not create 106 physical apartments.

[61] [62] [59]

Development phase

A separately identified part of a larger development. Track the phase name, unit count, address and status together.

Phoenix example: The Moreland is planned in two phases: 132 apartments in Phase I and 105 in Phase II.

Keep in mind: The 237 planned apartments are not a completed Phase I total.

[63] [64] [65]

Administrative completion

The completion date recorded for a program activity in the funding dataset.

Phoenix example: Phoenix’s Bell HOME activity records April 28, 2026; Arizona’s Bell activity records August 5, 2026.

Keep in mind: These are administrative activity dates, not verified construction, occupancy or payment dates.

[57] [58] [59] [60]

Authorization ceiling

The maximum amount an official action permits, often written as “up to.”

Phoenix example: Phoenix Council authorized up to $10 million from HOME, NSP and other affordable-housing funds for Moreland II on June 17, 2026.

Keep in mind: An authorization does not prove the amount was contracted or paid. The whole ceiling cannot be assigned to HOME without the fund split.

[66] [67]

Adopted operating budget

An approved financial plan for the named department and fiscal year.

Phoenix example: The Housing Department’s adopted FY2026–27 net operating expenditure budget is $207,589,044.

Keep in mind: This is budget authority, not actual spending or all housing-related activity across City departments.

[68]

Published award amount

The amount listed in an awarding agency’s published award record.

Phoenix example: HUD’s 2026 master Capital Fund ACC schedule lists $2,842,753 for Phoenix, grant AZ20P00150126.

Keep in mind: The public schedule does not establish Phoenix’s agreement execution, cash draws or expenditure.

[69] [70]

Formula calculation

A calculation used to determine funding. A separately published formula result may differ from another award record.

Phoenix example: HUD’s FY2026 formula workbook lists $2,813,904 for Phoenix.

Keep in mind: Development-level portions are formula inputs, not approved project budgets or project spending.

[2] [71]

Proposed uses

The planned purposes or amounts in a draft funding plan.

Phoenix example: Phoenix’s draft 2026–2030 Capital Fund plan shows $2,713,865 under the column labeled “2026.”

Keep in mind: The plan also says “2026/2027 CFP,” while a related action plan says “25-26 FY.” Those year labels remain unresolved.

[72] [73]

PHA obligation

In HUD’s Capital Fund guidance, a public housing agency’s binding agreement for work or financing that will result in outlays.

Phoenix example: An executed Phoenix work contract would help establish a PHA obligation; the published award amount alone does not.

Keep in mind: A PHA obligation differs from HUD’s federal award obligation. This definition is not evidence of a specific Phoenix transaction.

[74]

Grant draw

In HUD’s Capital Fund guidance, Capital Funds vouchered through its Line of Credit Control System, or LOCCS.

Phoenix example: A dated Phoenix draw record would help show movement of grant funds.

Keep in mind: A draw and a payment to a contractor or vendor are different steps. No Phoenix draw total is established by this definition.

[74]

HUD grant reporting expenditure

In HUD’s Capital Fund guidance, the PHA pays a contractor or vendor bill using funds disbursed from LOCCS.

Phoenix example: The relevant evidence would include Phoenix payment and grant records for the same grant and period.

Keep in mind: This program-reporting definition differs from the accounting measure in the SEFA.

[74] [75]

SEFA

The Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards reports federal-program expenditure for an accounting period.

Phoenix example: Phoenix’s FY2025 SEFA reports $7,218,862 for the Public Housing Capital Fund, ALN 14.872.

Keep in mind: The SEFA is not a cash-flow statement. This row has no grant-year breakdown, so subtracting a single annual award would not establish an unspent balance.

[75]

Total HOME Funding Amount

The named monetary field in HUD’s HOME activity data. Preserve that field label when reporting its value.

Phoenix example: Phoenix’s Bell activity reports $1,000,000; Arizona’s separate activity reports $1,082,181.40.

Keep in mind: The field does not independently establish a payment, a remaining loan balance or the development’s total cost.

[57] [58] [59] [60]

Conduit financing

Financing issued through a public issuer for the identified development. Read the issuer’s closing record separately from the prior authorization.

Phoenix example: Phoenix IDA reports $43.1 million of Moreland I financing closed in December 2025, following Council approval for up to $50 million.

Keep in mind: Do not add the two amounts or describe either as City spending or final development cost.

[76] [77] [78] [45]

Affordability restriction and expiry

The conditions and duration that govern a housing affordability commitment. The executed agreement and relevant amendments are needed to verify the applicable terms.

Phoenix example: Bell’s 2023 ARPA authorization described 11 units at or below 65% AMI for at least 20 years. The 2025 recovery report describes 16 units at or below 40% AMI.

Keep in mind: An executed amendment and current expiry date remain unverified. The older duration cannot simply be attached to the later unit and income statement.

[79] [80]

American Community Survey or ACS

A Census Bureau survey that produces estimates for a defined geography, year and population.

Phoenix example: The 2025 ACS one-year estimate of Phoenix city’s median gross monthly rent is $1,733.

Keep in mind: This is Phoenix city, not the metro area, and the estimate describes renter-occupied homes paying cash rent.

[81]

Median gross rent

The middle of the gross-rent distribution for the survey’s covered rental homes. Gross rent combines monthly contract rent with estimated average monthly costs for utilities and fuels.

Phoenix example: Phoenix city’s 2025 ACS estimate is $1,733 in nominal 2025 dollars.

Keep in mind: This is a survey measure of occupied rental housing, not today’s asking rent for available apartments.

[81] [82]

Margin of error or MOE

The survey’s measure of sampling uncertainty around an estimate, stated at a specified confidence level.

Phoenix example: The 2025 Phoenix city median gross rent estimate of $1,733 has a 90% margin of error of ±$36.

Keep in mind: The margin describes sampling uncertainty. It does not turn a citywide median into a price estimate for a particular apartment.

[81] [51]

Source coverage and retrieval date

Coverage describes the period a source says it represents. Retrieval is when the record was collected. Neither automatically proves when every underlying entry was refreshed.

Phoenix example: HUD’s HOME item states September 2026 coverage; the layer says December 2025 and Data.gov says June 2026. The retained records were retrieved November 8, 2026.

Keep in mind: The metadata disagree, and the exact refresh date has not been independently established.

[62] [59] [83] [84] [85]

HOME dataset coverage: The official HUD item states coverage of September 2026 and points to the same HOME service used for these activity records. The service-layer description says coverage through December 2025, while the Data.gov description says June 2026. These metadata descriptions disagree. The exact data-refresh date has not been independently established. All retained records were retrieved November 8, 2026.
