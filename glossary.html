Glossary of Terms and Research Identifiers

Housing programs use similar-sounding terms for different funding, ownership, and reporting processes. These brief definitions are provided for readers; the linked source records and applicable program rules remain authoritative.

Housing Programs and Reporting Abbreviations
TermMeaning in This Research
AAPAnnual Action Plan — a jurisdiction's plan for use of specified HUD community-development and housing funds.
AHAPAgreement to Enter into a Housing Assistance Payments Contract — an agreement connected with planned Section 8 project-based rental assistance, distinct from an executed HAP contract.
CAPERConsolidated Annual Performance and Evaluation Report — a report of program-year accomplishments and resources for HUD consolidated-plan programs.
CDBGCommunity Development Block Grant — federal funding for eligible community-development activities.
CoCContinuum of Care — a coordinated local system and federal funding program addressing homelessness and housing-related services.
CR-15A CAPER reporting section concerning resources and investments; amounts must be compared using consistent categories and periods.
ESGEmergency Solutions Grants — HUD funding for eligible emergency shelter, homelessness prevention, and rapid rehousing activities.
FY / PYFiscal Year / Program Year — reporting periods that may not use the same beginning and ending dates.
HAPHousing Assistance Payments — a contract or payment arrangement used in rental-assistance programs.
HCDHousing and Community Development — the City of Tucson department referenced in the source notices.
HCVHousing Choice Voucher — Section 8 tenant-based rental assistance generally used by an eligible household at a qualifying rental property; distinct from a project-based voucher.
HOMEHOME Investment Partnerships Program — federal affordable-housing funds. A HOME-designated unit is not an additional apartment on top of the project's total.
HOPWAHousing Opportunities for Persons With AIDS — a HUD housing-assistance program included in consolidated planning and reporting.
HUDU.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development.
IDISIntegrated Disbursement and Information System — HUD system used for grant activity and financial reporting.
PBVProject-Based Voucher — Section 8 rental assistance associated with specified apartments rather than a household-selected rental elsewhere.
PHAPublic Housing Agency — an organization administering eligible public housing or rental-assistance programs.
PR-26A HUD financial reporting reference used in the project's CDBG expenditure comparisons.
RADRental Assistance Demonstration — a HUD mechanism for converting eligible public housing assistance to longer-term rental assistance.
Section 8Federal rental-assistance authority, including Project-Based Vouchers. This is distinct from Section 18.
Section 18Public-housing demolition or disposition authority under the U.S. Housing Act of 1937; in the Craycroft notice it concerns disposition/conversion, not an additional voucher program.

How to Read Housing and Funding Figures

Allocated Is Not Available

An award, allocation, or proposed voucher commitment does not prove that a unit is vacant, a contract is executed, or an applicant can immediately receive assistance.

Unit Categories May Overlap

Project apartments, HOME-designated apartments, PBV-assisted apartments, and renovated apartments may describe the same physical homes. Do not add them together without a documented crosswalk.

Funding Measures Are Distinct

Planned allocations, commitments, expenditures, drawdowns, restricted carryforwards, and future rental-assistance estimates are not interchangeable cash balances.

Research Labels Are Not Verdicts

Source and finding IDs organize the evidence. An open question or reporting difference does not itself establish wrongdoing or noncompliance.

More precise reading habits

  • HAP has two meanings: Housing Accountability Project in this website’s name, and Housing Assistance Payments in rental-assistance records. A website reference label is not an agency code.
  • Affordability label: a source category or stated income/rent condition. The label alone does not establish an executed legal restriction or its end date.
  • Award, obligation, draw and expenditure: keep each financial stage separate. HUD Capital Fund guidance distinguishes a PHA agreement, funds vouchered through LOCCS and a vendor payment; the SEFA uses an accounting-period measure.
  • Coverage and retrieval: the period a dataset says it covers differs from the date it was collected. Neither automatically verifies when every underlying row was refreshed.

Public website labels and official codes are different

Housing Accountability Project assigns source, finding, question and public-card labels to organize the website’s evidence. A label is not an agency certification. An official program or grant identifier must be read with its agency, jurisdiction and period.

Code familyHow to read it
FUND-PUB-*A curated funding-summary card in the public research extract. It is a Housing Accountability Project tracking ID, not an agency grant number or financial statement. Public example: FUND-PUB-001
PROJECT-PUB-*A curated project card in the public research extract. It does not establish a legal parcel, owner or current vacancy. Public example: PROJECT-PUB-001
RECORD-PUB-*A curated records-request card. Its identifier does not establish that a request was sent, answered or approved. Public example: RECORD-PUB-001
Phoenix PHX-* labelsSource-reference labels used in the public Phoenix research area. They are distinct from Tucson’s public source labels and from official agency identifiers. Public example: PHX-CITY-002 is a source reference in the public Phoenix index.
