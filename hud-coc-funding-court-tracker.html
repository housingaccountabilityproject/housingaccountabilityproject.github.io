Who Gets HUD Homelessness Funding? The Court Fight and Arizona’s Next Steps

A federal court ruled on parts of HUD’s 2026 funding competition. Arizona’s local programs have announced next steps, but those announcements do not tell us who will be funded.

What happened and why it matters

HUD’s Continuum of Care program funds organizations and public agencies that provide housing and services to people experiencing homelessness. The FY2026 competition groups projects in two tiers. Local Continuums of Care rank projects; eligible higher-priority projects generally go first, and projects in Tier 2 compete nationally.

Two groups challenged parts of the competition. One lawsuit was brought by Washington and other states, including Arizona. The other was brought by the National Alliance to End Homelessness and other plaintiffs, including the City of Tucson. The challengers argued that HUD’s rules favored certain new projects over existing permanent housing. HUD argued that Congress gave it discretion to add selection criteria and that other eligible projects could still compete for the remaining money.

The District Court in Rhode Island concluded that HUD exceeded its authority by using a $1.3 billion set-aside in Tier 2 to prioritize certain new transitional housing and supportive-services-only projects ahead of other projects. It ruled for each side on different provisions. The court’s reasoning matters because it addressed whether existing permanent housing projects got to compete under criteria set by Congress. The $1.3 billion is a national figure in a challenged competition rule, not an Arizona award or reported spending.

Read the court’s written reasoning, including the set-aside and HUD’s defense.

Court timeline

  1. June–July 2026: HUD issued the FY2026 competition notice in June. The two lawsuits were filed in July. Court’s account of the filings.
  2. August 7: The district court set aside the funding notice in an earlier ruling. This was not the last ruling. Court’s procedural history.
  3. September 16 and 24: The First Circuit paused the August ruling on September 16. On September 24 it set aside the earlier judgment and returned the cases to the district court. First Circuit stay opinion · September 24 judgment.
  4. November 20: The district court issued a new written opinion addressing both cases. It set aside specific provisions, including the Tier 2 set-aside, rather than the entire competition notice. Written opinion and disposition.
  5. November 23: A text order in the Washington case set aside the separate November 20 judgment. It names that judgment; it does not state that the written opinion was withdrawn. A November 23 order is also listed in the companion case, but HAP has not verified what it says. Washington docket entries 56–57 and November 23 entry.

What Arizona programs announced

Tucson & Pima County

The Tucson Pima Collaboration to End Homelessness says its board decided on November 23 to allow a limited round of local revisions. The decision affects nine projects from four partner agencies that had proposed transitional housing and could instead seek to renew existing rapid rehousing or permanent supportive housing projects. TPCH said the local process was tentative while it awaited further HUD instructions.

TPCH’s local competition notice

Phoenix & Maricopa County

The Maricopa Regional Continuum of Care says its board approved a limited reopening of the local Tier 2 competition for currently funded permanent-housing projects seeking renewal. This is a local grant-application step.

Maricopa’s local competition notice

Neither announcement confirms a federal submission, announces an award, or opens a housing application for residents.

What remains open

We have not verified the text of the companion November 23 order, current federal submission instructions, revised local submissions, or local awards from this ruling. We’ll record each as a separate step when there’s a public source for it. The timelines above are for tracking evidence, not for filing applications.
