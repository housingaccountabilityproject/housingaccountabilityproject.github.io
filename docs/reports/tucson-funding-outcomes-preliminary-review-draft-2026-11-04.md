**Housing Accountability Project**  
**Draft prepared:** November 4, 2026  
**Research periods:** Primarily PY2024 and the PY2025 public-comment draft, with selected 2026 decisions and housing milestones  
**Status:** Source-updated editorial draft (Revision 0.2, November 4, 2026) — **not approved for public publication**

> **Publication note.** This document summarizes selected existing public records and the project's research registers. Its numerical comparisons are limited to their stated reporting periods and categories. It does not establish currently available housing assistance, program misconduct, discrimination, or regulatory noncompliance.

## Executive summary

The City of Tucson and its regional partners administer or participate in multiple housing-related programs. Public reports identify financial investments, changes in planned funding, completed or renovated housing, and continuing activities. Yet a reader cannot assume that an award is cash received, that a carryforward is unobligated money, or that an allocated voucher represents an apartment available to a new applicant.

The Housing Accountability Project reviewed selected City reports, HUD-related reporting references, City announcements, and the project's cross-referenced research records. The review establishes several practical distinctions:

1. **Reported spending needs accounting context.** The City's PY2024 CDBG resource subtotal includes program income. The corrected PR-26 financial summary is present within the published CAPER PDF (pages 148–150), resolving an earlier source-location gap. A separate difference between CR-15 and narrative figures remains an *unreconciled reporting-scope comparison*, not a finding that money is missing.
2. **Reallocations are not new awards.** A City Council communication reports an October 2026 shift of $1.5 million in planned CDBG funds toward housing-related activities. The shift does not add $1.5 million to the federal award.
3. **Housing outcomes have stages.** Public announcements identify apartments opened, motel units converted, buildings under construction, and existing units undergoing preservation. These stages should not be combined into a single total of new or vacant apartments.
4. **Final reporting remains a priority.** The publicly examined PY2025 CAPER is a draft referencing tables intended for the final submission. The final HUD-submitted version, related schedules, and review history were not verified in the project's November 3 source snapshot.
5. **The record contains achievements as well as questions.** Reported completed housing and rehabilitations deserve recognition alongside requests for clearer expenditure, contract, occupancy, and outcome documentation.

This is a preliminary research assessment. Further original records and agency explanations may revise the observations below.

## 1. Scope, sources, and interpretation

The review distinguishes **Program Year 2024** reporting from the **Program Year 2025** public-comment draft, selected **2026 City decisions**, and planning figures for later periods. Program-year and municipal fiscal-year labels are identified where available.

The principal document families are the City's CAPER publications, Annual Action Plans, dated housing news and council communications, HUD/IDIS reporting references, and project financial and performance material cited in the project's public evidence register.

The public Research Center is a curated subset of the larger research effort. The project maintains separate internal master and accountability audit workbooks, but those workbooks contain working notes and source limitations and are **not** automatically released as public documents.

All dollar amounts below should be read with their **program, jurisdiction, year, and accounting type**. No cross-program sum is presented as uncommitted housing funds or spendable balances.

## 2. CDBG: funding resources, expenditures, and category changes

### PY2024 resource presentation

In the project's review of the City of Tucson's PY2024 CAPER, the CDBG financial presentation shows a **$5,418,132 current-resource subtotal**, including **$349,468 in program income**. The underlying comparison base of $5,068,664 plus program income reconciles to the subtotal.

**Interpretation:** The program-income component is already part of the reported resource total and should not be added again. This arithmetic reconciliation does not establish the current availability of any remaining resources.

### Expenditure comparison requiring explanation

The project's PY2024 CAPER review records a **$4,717,936** CDBG expenditure figure in the CR-15 table, while narrative figures separately state **$3,433,346.66** for CDBG and **$339,616.74** for CDBG-CV.

The arithmetic comparison is:

```text
$4,717,936.00
− $3,433,346.66
−   $339,616.74
=   $944,972.60
```

**This is an arithmetic difference between published presentations, not a verified accounting discrepancy or missing-money finding.** The figures may use different program, adjustment, or reporting scopes.

**Primary-source correction (November 4, 2026):** The City's full PY2024 CAPER PDF *does* include an appended corrected Tucson PR-26 Financial Summary Report, on **PDF page 149 (printed CAPER page 146)**, dated **March 13, 2026**. The report shows **$4,717,936.37** in total expenditures, **$349,468.00** in current-year CDBG program income, and **$5,418,132.00** as the current entitlement-plus-program-income basis used for the planning/administration cap calculation. PDF page **148** contains the City's narrative explaining a **$102,106.09 public-service expense coding adjustment from PY2024 back to PY2023**; the City states its HUD field representative reviewed and approved the adjustment. That is a City-authored claim in the CAPER, not a separately reviewed HUD determination.

The more precise, source-located PR-26 expenditure figure supports a *different arithmetic comparison* with the standard CDBG narrative alone:

```text
$4,717,936.37 corrected PR-26 total expenditures
− $3,433,346.66 standard CDBG narrative expenditures
= $1,284,589.71 arithmetic difference, excluding CDBG-CV narrative
```

The difference is mathematically reproducible, but **the two measures are not established as like-for-like accounting scopes**. The comparison with both CDBG and CDBG-CV narrative amounts, using the rounded CR-15 table figure, remains **$944,972.60**. Neither comparison proves a shortfall, unspent funding, improper use, or fraud.

**What remains open is no longer locating the included PR-26**. It is matching the reporting scopes and calculation bridge, including CDBG-CV classification, period cutoffs, activity-level IDIS draw entries, and any separate report versions outside this CAPER attachment.

### October 2026 CDBG reallocation

A City Council member's October 9, 2026 update reports that Mayor and Council approved adjusting the FY2027/PY2026 HUD Annual Action Plan to shift **$1.5 million of planned CDBG funding** from Community Facilities toward Decent Affordable Rental and Homeowner Housing.

This is a **category reallocation**: one planned category decreases while another increases. It does not establish a new federal award or project expenditures. Verification still requires the final legal action and amended plan, HUD/IDIS implementation records, project commitments, actual draws, and resulting housing work.

## 3. HOME: reporting categories, carryforwards, and designated units

The project's PY2024 CAPER analysis records approximately **$3,148,083 in HOME resources** and **$479,243 in expenditures**. Both are historical, rounded report measures. Subtracting the two figures cannot establish currently unobligated funds because project commitments, draw dates, restrictions, and period cutoffs are not resolved in the summary.

The City's **draft PY2025 CAPER** separately reports:

| Draft measure | Reported amount | What it does *not* establish |
|---|---:|---|
| HOME program-income carryforward | $527,244.29 | Currently uncommitted or immediately available assistance |
| HOME recaptured-funds carryforward | $307,564.13 | An unrestricted rental subsidy pool |
| Nine open HOME activities | 44 designated HOME units | 44 available or newly occupied apartments |
| Nine activity amounts, combined | $5,470,000 | A verified expenditure total or present grant balance |

The project's arithmetic on the two carryforwards checks against figures presented in the draft. Their **restrictions and actual activity-level commitments remain open**. Designated HOME units may be a subset of broader developments; they are not an additional count of apartments. Final CAPER schedules and underlying IDIS activity records are needed before making cost-per-unit or availability claims.

## 4. ESG and HOPWA: measurable outcomes and supporting reports

The PY2024 ESG rapid-rehousing table on **CAPER PDF page 9** shows **30 planned households and 18 actual households**. The source material also contains a separately dated grant-draw report. These measures should not be divided together to infer an exact cost per household or treated as proof that an agency failed to serve particular applicants.

The City's **PY2024 CAPER PDF page 41** directly reports a HOPWA household table with **248 one-year goal households and 124 actual households across four housing assistance categories**; its categories and definitions must be retained when discussing targets. The same CAPER's CR-15 table reports **$1,054,399 in HOPWA resources** and **$858,254 in expenditures** (PDF page 13). The fuller *separate* HOPWA CAPER referenced in the annual report was not independently inspected. These reported household counts and financial measures should not be divided or compared without matched cohorts and activity costs.

A useful public assessment requires comparable expenditure periods, clear program categories, relevant outcome cohorts, and any available agency explanation of differences between targets and reported results.

## 5. Housing developments: documented stages, not vacancy estimates

The following examples demonstrate the importance of distinguishing housing development, preservation, and applicant access:

| Project | City-reported milestone | Limits on interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Milagro on Oracle** | 63 affordable apartments reported opened in January 2025 | Not a dated vacancy or independently audited current occupancy count |
| **Amazon Motel** | Conversion of an existing motel into 30 supportive-housing studios reported completed in September 2025 | Separate from Amazon Flats; not a current referral or vacancy list |
| **Amazon Flats** | Groundbreaking in April 2026 for 59 apartments; earlier notice proposed 39 PBVs and two HOME-designated units | Construction is not completion; subsidies and units overlap and cannot be added |
| **Craycroft Towers / Lee Street Duplexes** | Notices describe 74 plus eight **existing** apartments and 82 allocated project-based vouchers | Allocation does not confirm executed HAP contracts, vacancies, or newly constructed housing |
| **Tucson House** | October 2026 announcement described 24 renovated apartments on the 15th floor and anticipated movement of 25 households | Rehabilitation is not new construction; anticipated moves are not confirmed completed admissions |

These announcements identify substantive housing activity, including new construction, adaptive reuse, supportive housing, and preservation. What remains missing for a full community outcomes assessment is a consistent project-by-project record of completion dates, unique unit populations, occupancy, tenancy duration, and qualifying program assistance.

## 6. Housing Choice Vouchers and separate accounting scopes

The project's research questions examine a City narrative of approximately **$62.8 million in HCV funding** alongside separately presented Tucson and Pima County payment figures. These figures may represent different fiscal periods, public housing authority jurisdictions, funding categories, and expenditure definitions.

No responsible inference about remaining voucher assistance or issuance capacity can be made from their simple subtraction. An analysis would require separate PHA records for actual receipts, Housing Assistance Payments, administrative fees, reserves and restrictions, contractual obligations, and dated lease-up forecasts.

Likewise, a planned or allocated project-based voucher does not by itself establish an available unit or applicant eligibility pathway.

## 7. CAPER status and outstanding primary records

The City's publicly reviewed **PY2025 CAPER public-comment draft** identifies some financial and performance tables as items to be included in a final report. The research snapshot does not independently establish whether HUD received or accepted a final CAPER or whether additional schedules were subsequently published.

The project reported submitting a **HUD FOIA request through the online portal on November 3, 2026**, seeking the final submission, selected supporting records, and HUD review history. The report does not assert a verified agency acknowledgment, document production, or FOIA tracking number.

This uncertainty is about the **records available for this research**, not evidence that the City failed to meet a filing obligation.

## 9. Research standards and corrections

The Housing Accountability Project follows these rules:

- Distinguish official agency statements, directly checked records, workbook calculations, proposed plans, and unresolved questions.
- Preserve the accounting measure, jurisdiction, reporting year, source title, page reference where available, and verification date.
- Avoid adding figures from different grant programs, periods, or reporting categories into a claimed "housing money available" total.
- Distinguish project apartments from subsidized or renovated units, housing completions from construction starts, and historical milestones from current vacancies.
- Incorporate relevant agency responses and documented successes, and issue dated corrections when material wording or values change.
- Exclude individual case files, private correspondence, health information, and personal identifiers from public research publications.

**Publication boundary:** This is an independent review of selected records; it is **not** a certified financial audit, a complete census of housing assistance, an assessment of individual eligibility, or a conclusion about legal liability.

## Preliminary conclusion

Existing records describe meaningful housing activity in Tucson while leaving important questions about financial presentation, project-level spending, voucher implementation, and comparable housing outcomes unanswered.

The next contribution of the Housing Accountability Project should be to make those questions **more specific and easier to verify**: identify the exact documents required, publish reproducible source comparisons, recognize confirmed results, and revise conclusions when fuller records become available.

**Public funding deserves public understanding.**

*Follow the funding. Understand the outcomes.*
