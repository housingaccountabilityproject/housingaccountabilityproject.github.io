How to interpret these findings

This page presents selected, source-linked observations, not an official financial audit, a finding of wrongdoing, or a live housing-availability service.

Source reviewed confirms a limited statement in an agency publication, not implementation or an independently audited outcome. Financial categories and project-unit categories are not additive unless their definitions and periods match.

Three evidence levels: Source reviewed — an agency publication was examined for the stated fact. Workbook assessment — an internal comparison was documented but not fully reproduced from primary records. Open question — additional information is needed before reaching a conclusion.

Source reviewed F-001 · CDBG · PY2026

City approved a $1.5 million CDBG reallocation

The City's October 9 update reports that Mayor and Council approved shifting $1.5 million in previously planned CDBG funding within the FY2027/PY2026 Annual Action Plan.

The City reported moving the allocation from Community Facilities to Decent Affordable Rental and Homeowner Housing, including potential preservation or rehabilitation activities. The housing category gains $1.5 million in planned capacity while the facilities category loses the same amount. Total CDBG resources do not increase.

Housing category: +$1,500,000
Facilities category: −$1,500,000
Net change in total CDBG funding from this reallocation: $0
What this does not prove HUD implementation, unobligated cash, a new federal award, completed rehabilitation, or housing openings.
Source: City Council member update, October 9, 2026 · Q-006
Source reviewed F-002 · PBV / HOME · 2025–2026

Amazon Flats: construction started; vouchers proposed

The City announced an April 17, 2026 groundbreaking for a 59-apartment building. A December 2025 environmental notice proposed using 39 project-based vouchers.

The earlier notice described $500,000 in HOME funds and two HOME-designated units within the 59 planned apartments. The City estimated completion in fall 2027.

Do not add 59 + 39 + 2. These refer to overlapping apartment and subsidy categories, not separate sets of homes.

Financing classification: The City reported an overall development cost of approximately $24.4 million. The earlier environmental notice identified a proposed $500,000 HOME contribution and estimated $6,645,600 in PBV assistance over 20 years.

These figures describe development cost, a proposed capital source, and projected future rental assistance. They do not establish a combined balance of currently available housing funds.

What this does not prove Construction completion, executed voucher contracts, current applications, or vacant apartments.
Sources: December 22, 2025 funding notice · April 17, 2026 groundbreaking · Q-009
Source reviewed F-003 · PBV / RAD · August 2026

Craycroft and Lee Street: allocated vouchers

The City's August 3, 2026 notice describes 74 existing Craycroft Towers apartments and eight Lee Street Duplex apartments, with 82 project-based vouchers allocated over 15 years.

The notice identifies nine Rental Assistance Demonstration (RAD) units and 65 Section 18 disposition units at Craycroft Towers. Together, these classifications cover its 74 existing apartments. The notice also identifies four HOME-assisted units within the project.

Understanding Section 18 versus Section 8: Section 18 concerns the disposition of public housing as part of a conversion or preservation transaction. Section 8 is a separate rental-assistance authority, including Project-Based Vouchers (PBVs). The City reports an allocation of 82 Section 8 PBVs for the combined Craycroft Towers (74 apartments) and Lee Street Duplexes (8 apartments), over 15 years. These figures describe overlapping units and program classifications; they are not additional homes. See the terminology guide.

The work concerns preservation and conversion of existing housing, not construction of 82 new apartments. An allocation is not proof that voucher contracts are executed or that apartments are vacant.

What this does not prove Executed assistance-payment agreements, completed conversion, current occupancy, or 82 vacant apartments.
Source: City project notice, August 3, 2026 · Q-009
Source reviewed F-004 · Housing delivery · 2025

Milagro on Oracle: documented housing delivery

The City reports that this 63-unit affordable housing development opened to residents in January 2025.

City announcements credit El Pueblo Housing Development and Gorman & Company with developing this housing, combining new construction and adaptive reuse of two historic motor courts.

This is a documented positive housing-delivery milestone, although the City announcement is not an independently audited occupancy report.

What this does not prove Current vacancies, present occupancy, resident income levels, or remaining available subsidy.
Sources: City announcement, August 2025 · City co-developer recognition, August 2026

PY2025 CAPER: public-comment stage documented

The 33-page draft indicates that certain CR-05, CR-10, and CR-15 tables would be available in the final report. Their absence from the draft does not establish that the City failed to file a final CAPER.

What remains unverified The final CAPER, submission date, HUD receipt or acceptance, and supporting appendices. Failure to locate a record does not prove non-submission.

Draft arithmetic checked F-009 · HOME · PY2025

HOME carryforwards: amounts and limitations

The PY2025 draft reports two separate HOME carryforward figures, not a verified current subsidy balance.

Program income: $534,792.63 received − $7,548.34 applied = $527,244.29 carryforward.

Recaptured funds: $373,430.46 received − $65,866.33 applied = $307,564.13 carryforward.

Limits and uses The draft states that program income supported tenant-based rental assistance and recaptured funds supported down-payment assistance. Neither year-end figure proves currently unobligated cash or eligibility for any particular household. HOME matching credits are a separate noncash category.
Source: Draft CAPER, PDF page 26 · Q-011

Nine HOME activities: 44 designated units

The PY2025 draft lists nine open HOME activities whose designated HOME-unit counts total 44.

The nine listed project amounts total $5,470,000. These are activity amounts, not independently confirmed expenditures or a present grant balance.

What this does not prove That 44 designated HOME units equal all apartments, that units are currently occupied or available, or that $5.47 million has been fully spent.

Source reviewed F-011 · Supportive housing · 2025

Amazon Motel: a separate 30-apartment project

The City reports that the former Amazon Motel was converted into 30 permanent supportive housing studio apartments, completed in September 2025.

Amazon Motel is an existing-building conversion, distinct from Amazon Flats, the separate 59-apartment development on the adjoining site.

What this does not prove Current resident numbers, vacancies, retention rates, or apartments available for new referrals.
Source: City April 2026 announcement · Amazon Flats distinction
Source reviewed F-012 · Housing preservation · October 2026

Tucson House: renovation milestone

On October 16, 2026, the City reported 24 renovated apartments on Tucson House's 15th floor and stated that 25 households would be moving into renovated housing.

The work involves rehabilitation of existing public housing, not 24 newly constructed apartments. The broader project concerns 358 apartments, with further renovation milestones anticipated through summer 2028.

The difference between 24 apartments and 25 households requires clarification, not an assumption of error.

What this does not prove That all 25 households already moved by October 16, that these were newly admitted applicants, or that apartments remain available for external referrals.
Source: City Tucson House announcement, October 16, 2026

Open source reconciliation F-007 · CDBG / CDBG-CV · PY2024

Why two CDBG reporting differences appear

The City's PY2024 CAPER, CR-15 (PDF page 13, printed page 10), reports $4,717,936 in CDBG expenditures in its table. Its narrative separately lists $3,433,346.66 for CDBG and $339,616.74 for CDBG-CV.

CR-15 table compared with both narrative amounts: $4,717,936.00 − $3,433,346.66 − $339,616.74 = $944,972.60

An earlier workbook extract also references a purported corrected PR-26 expenditure of $4,717,936.37. Subtracting only the standard CDBG narrative amount, without the separate CDBG-CV figure, produces $1,284,589.71. The original Tucson PR-26 report was not located in the source review, so that more precise figure remains workbook-reported, not independently verified against the PR-26.

Provisional workbook comparison (CDBG narrative only): $4,717,936.37 − $3,433,346.66 = $1,284,589.71

These are different arithmetic comparisons, not two verified measures of missing funds. Even the $944,972.60 cross-presentation difference may mix CDBG and CDBG-CV reporting scopes, and cannot be treated as a reconciled accounting balance. No available comparison establishes improper spending or unrestricted funding.

Next verification Obtain the original Tucson PY2024 PR-26 report and any corrected runs, the CR-15 calculation workpaper, activity-level IDIS draw records, report-generation dates, and an explanation of how CDBG-CV is classified. Preserve the different reporting definitions before attempting a transaction-level reconciliation.
Sources: City PY2024 CAPER, CR-15 (PDF p. 13) · Q-007 · SRC-023 · Research Updates

Methodology and correction policy

Every finding should be reproducible using a document title, reporting period, source page, quantitative inputs, and a documented interpretation.

Plans, awards, commitments, expenditures, project costs, available cash, new apartments, rehabilitated apartments, occupied apartments, HOME-designated units, and PBV-supported units are different measures.

This project documents legitimate agency achievements and possible explanations alongside discrepancies. An unresolved research question is not an allegation of misconduct or noncompliance.

No private housing, medical, court, or personal correspondence is published in this page.

Correction policy

Material revisions will preserve the finding ID and identify the previous wording, revised statement, reason for the correction, supporting records, and correction date.
